# Short-form clip research

For TikTok content gaps, creator research or performance, use Project `tiktok-content-research` and its platform-specific measures.

Use public pages and material the user supplies or can access for free. Work with plain text, a table, and manual playback. The workflow needs zero purchases, subscriptions, trials, paid assets, API keys, extensions, or specific software. When playback or analytics are unavailable, use the relevant fallback below and carry the evidence gap into the output.

For independent creator channels, follow the creator-clipping playbook. Public examples can inform ideas and comparisons. For an actual cut, work within footage and moments the human selected and supplied. Use the authorized footage only after reuse is cleared for the intended edit. Keep unresolved footage as a research reference and route rights questions to Partnerships. Release follows the workspace approval policy.

## Research ideas and find moments

1. Establish the audience, the target format, and what success means for this request. Infer reasonable defaults when unspecified. For source clips, identify the strongest moment a new viewer can understand and explain in one sentence.
2. For inspiration, manually inspect a small sample of relevant channels. Log the URL, format, publication date, visible views, observation date, and a short description of the idea, opening, and payoff. Sample ordinary uploads as well as breakouts. Keep Shorts and long-form uploads in separate cohorts.
3. Estimate a breakout multiple only when same-format, similar-age counts are available. Divide the candidate's view count by the median of comparable uploads on that channel, excluding the candidate. Prefer a fixed window since publication when historical snapshots exist. Otherwise use a similar-age group observed on the same date, mark the result approximate, and give the group size and age range. Report the counts and omit the ratio when there is only one eligible peer, the cohort is otherwise too thin to support it, or its median is zero. A standout is a lead for creative research. Record the proposed reason as a hypothesis plus one plausible competing explanation.
4. Borrow the underlying mechanism, such as a visible reversal, a surprising comparison, a question resolved on screen, or evidence revealed after a prediction. Use original wording, visuals, and framing. Log source links and revisit promising topics as needed.
5. For each source, verify timestamps against the transcript or playback and record which you used. Verify names, numbers, quotes, and visuals on playback when accessible. Use caption-only times for draft cut notes. Mark them approximate and describe unseen visual beats conditionally. Confirm exact boundaries on supplied footage before editing. If the source has neither playable footage nor timed text, use untimed candidate descriptions or search cues. Keep third-party footage, music, and reuse status visible in the cut sheet. Mark unresolved clearance as research-only pending Partnerships review.

## Rank and edit clips

- Score each candidate from 0 to 2 on five editorial criteria. **Opening** communicates something specific immediately. **Standalone clarity** supplies just enough context. **Payoff** visibly or audibly resolves the opening. **Audience relevance** matches the target viewer. **Editability** reflects usable footage or audio and a coherent cut. Score 0 for absent or weak evidence, 1 for partial, 2 for clear evidence. Use a range when evidence is missing, for example 6–8/10 with opening visuals unverified. State that the score prioritizes cuts and carries no probability of virality.
- Draft two openings for the strongest cut when useful. Lead with an accurate claim, action, or visual and make the stake legible in the first beat. Preserve qualifiers, chronology, and the speaker's meaning. Front-load enough context to orient the viewer. Trim dead air based on the actual story and keep the complete payoff. Let the content determine duration, with a tighter version as a testable option.
- If the clip is meant to reach beyond regular viewers, check whether a first-time viewer can explain what is at stake and why the outcome matters without knowing the creator or niche. Give them a clear visual, question or decision drawn from the footage. Keep specialist language when the intended audience already knows it.
- Choose a title that conveys the real promise in natural language. Use a relevant hashtag only if it helps categorize the topic. Put the research effort into audience fit, premise, and edit. For science or AI clips, trace consequential claims to the original study or demonstration and keep the relevant limit in the script. For third-party footage, make the editorial contribution explicit through original commentary, context, or storytelling.
- Return a ranked checklist or compact table with source and time range when known, timestamp status, opening line or action, setup and payoff, cut notes, editorial score, and clearance status. Link time ranges when the source has a usable URL. Leave endpoints unknown when the evidence ends mid-beat. Time noncontiguous beats individually and state the editing order. Include before-publication checks for source accuracy, captions, and clearance.

## Diagnose published Shorts

Compare the same format, similar duration and audience, and the same age since publication. Use a recent matched channel baseline. Record public views as playback volume, which can include repeat plays. Use shown-in-feed or unique reach when available to evaluate exposure. Use owner-supplied engagement data to diagnose the cut, when available. Record the reporting window, traffic source mix, and sample size wherever supplied. Treat sparse data and mixed definitions as limitations.

Treat a recurring view plateau as an observation about that channel. Public counts alone cannot establish a recommendation cap, a policy penalty or the location of an editing problem.

| Pattern relative to the channel baseline | Next check or experiment |
| --- | --- |
| Little feed exposure or too few observations | Check whether the Short is public, available and unrestricted in Studio when the owner has access. Compare Shorts Feed traffic and feed exposure when available. Keep the result provisional and measure again at the planned checkpoint. |
| Weak stayed-to-watch with typical duration among engaged viewers | Try a clearer opening frame, first line, or statement of stakes on the next comparable clips. |
| Typical stayed-to-watch with weaker average duration or percentage viewed | When a retention graph is available, locate a candidate drop and rewatch that beat. Before payoff, check for missing context, a slow setup or a broken promise. After payoff, check whether a tail or CTA adds value. A drop after delivery may be expected. |
| Strong viewing metrics with modest reach | Check audience fit, topic demand, traffic mix, and observational uncertainty before changing the edit. |
| Public view counts only | Describe relative playback volume and editorial observations. Ask for first-party data only if the user wants a diagnosis that depends on it. |

Read stayed-to-watch or chose-to-view as the early choice signal. Read engaged views, average view duration, and average percentage viewed together. Average duration and percentage viewed for Shorts are based on engaged views and their watch time. A public view count includes starts and replays. A retention graph, when available, can help locate candidate trouble spots. Treat a dip as a prompt to rewatch the segment.

## Run a free experiment

- Before posting, define a hypothesis, the one edit variable to change, a primary metric, matched comparison clips, and a review window. For example, test a result-first opening across a batch of similar original clips and measure stayed-to-watch seven days after each upload. Use similar topics, durations, and posting conditions where practical. Keep a secondary metric such as average view duration or percentage viewed to check for tradeoffs. The seven-day example is an observation window, not a ranking rule.
- Separate a topic or audience test from a presentation test. For example, hold the audience and premise steady when comparing relevant gameplay with a static visual. Hold the format steady when testing a narrower topic. If both change together, record that the result cannot isolate either one.
- Preview two versions with a few willing viewers when possible. Ask what each clip promises and whether the payoff satisfied that promise. Use feedback to catch confusion. Label comparisons across distinct published Shorts as observational, even when paired. Formal concurrent packaging tests apply to eligible long-form videos. Shorts opening tests can be judged through previews and patterns across distinct clips.
- Keep the original upload and its observations. Choose a cadence sustainable for the creator. Base the next change on repeatable patterns. Record inconclusive results when exposure is low, comparison clips differ materially, or metrics disagree.

Before diagnosing a metric or relying on a platform feature, consult [Evidence and claim review](references/evidence.md) and verify current platform documentation when material.
