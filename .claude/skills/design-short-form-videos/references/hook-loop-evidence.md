# Evidence for hooks and loops

The studies below inform design choices. Their outcomes include information seeking, recall and comprehension in specific tasks. Watch time, replay preference, and platform distribution require their own measurements.

| Principle | What the source supports | Production use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity and information gaps | Loewenstein proposed that recognizing a gap in understanding can motivate curiosity. In Kang et al.'s trivia experiments, higher curiosity ratings were associated with a greater willingness to spend scarce tokens or wait for answers. | Give the viewer a concrete missing piece and a reason to care. The token and waiting tasks do not estimate how people behave in a feed. [Loewenstein](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf) · [Kang et al.](https://mcclurelab.org/pdf/Kang2009.pdf) |
| Prequestions | The meta-analysis found a moderate average benefit for the material actually prequestioned and a near-zero average effect for untested material. | Ask a prediction about the exact point the video will explain. A prequestion gives no basis for promising broad learning gains or a fixed pause length. [St Hilaire et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37640836/) |
| Prediction error | In a controlled experiment, unexpected interruptions to familiar clips altered episodic memory updating, including later memory intrusions. The supplied PMID 37636962 is a chemistry paper and cannot support this principle. | Show the actual expected and observed outcomes when the mismatch helps explain an event. Describe surprise as a way to frame the contrast. Memory updating can include confusion, and the study does not measure hook effectiveness. [Sinclair et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8713973/) · [Original mismatched citation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37636962/) |
| Self-reference | A meta-analysis found a memory advantage for self-referential encoding over comparison tasks, with effect size varying by the comparison. | Anchor a real explanation in a familiar action or decision. The evidence concerns memory under studied conditions. [Symons and Johnson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9136641/) |
| Curiosity and memory | Gruber et al. grouped trivia trials using participants' curiosity ratings and found better recall for high-curiosity answers. fMRI signals in regions associated with reward and memory were related to the effect. | A meaningful question is a reasonable setup. Describe the comparison as rating-based and the brain finding as fMRI activity. Avoid claims about directly measured dopamine, a viewer's brain state, or retention uplift. [Gruber et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/) |
| Relevant detail | A multilevel meta-analysis estimated a small average learning cost for interesting material irrelevant to the instruction, with variation by setting. | Keep a surprising insert when it helps the viewer interpret the evidence. Use visual explanation where an educational cut needs to support comprehension. [Cheng et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10099-z) |
| Action continuity and event boundaries | Tests using film excerpts found that continuity could make some cuts harder to detect and that action changes strongly affected perceived event boundaries. Other experiments found that object memory depended on where items appeared relative to those boundaries. | Preserve a meaningful action at a hidden join and use clear internal transitions where a step or concept changes. These studies measured edit detection, segmentation or memory. [Smith and Henderson](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2264) · [Magliano and Zacks](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2011.01202.x) · [Swallow et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2819197/) |
| Unfinished tasks and repeated viewing | A meta-analysis found evidence of task resumption and no overall recall advantage for unfinished tasks. Repeated viewing experiments reported changes in perceived rehearsal and spontaneity. | Resolve the payoff in the available cut. When an authentic reaction matters, check how it reads on repeat. These results cover specific tasks and judgments. [Ghibellini and Meier](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w) · [Donnelly et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976241242462) |

## Interpret replay evidence

- YouTube says Shorts ranking considers the choice to watch, viewing duration and percentage, and satisfaction signals. Studio's **Stayed to watch** describes viewing past the initial seconds. Use it with first-beat comprehension for opening checks. Use average view duration, average percentage viewed and retention near the payoff where those metrics are available. [Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225) · [YouTube Analytics definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- Public Shorts views count starts and replays with no minimum watch time. Engaged views exclude loops and use a different viewing threshold. The difference between these counts includes brief starts and cannot estimate replay rate. A retention spike can reflect a replay, a seek, comprehension difficulty, or a useful moment. Look for corroborating viewer feedback or a controlled preview. [YouTube view-count update](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549) · [Audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- YouTube's native title and thumbnail A/B testing excludes Shorts. When testing an opening, keep the body, payoff and ending stable. When testing a replay join, vary the ending treatment and retain the same opening and payoff. Keep duration comparable where practical and log meaningful differences. Compare like posts at matched post ages and call the published comparison observational. TikTok tests use the measures and traffic-source guidance in `tiktok-content-research`. [YouTube A/B test eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)
- A vendor reported higher replay, reach and engagement for looping brand Facebook Reels up to seven seconds in its dataset. Its stated replay definition includes repeated segments. Treat the numbers as observational and tied to that cohort and definition. For a stronger private comparison, randomly assign viewers to otherwise matched versions, predefine the outcome, and measure whether they got the first-play answer as well as any decision to replay. [Emplifi Facebook Reels analysis](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
