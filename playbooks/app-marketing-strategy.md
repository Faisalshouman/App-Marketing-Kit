# Marketing strategy by app type

Start with one audience, one repeatable job, one channel, and one measured first-value event. Select the closest row and adapt it to your business model and platform.

| App type | Acquisition starting point | First value | Follow-up signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer web/mobile | Discoverable demos, app-store listing, community or creator fit | Complete the core activity | Repeat use and meaningful retention |
| B2B SaaS | Problem-specific search, partner referrals, demo or trial | Complete a representative team workflow | Adopt a second workflow or invite a teammate |
| Developer tools/API | Docs, runnable example, integration catalogue | First successful real request or integration | Repeat successful use and error rate |
| Marketplace | Focus on one category and geography, seed the constrained side | First qualified match or completed transaction | Repeat matches, fulfillment and disputes |
| Desktop/creative | Before-and-after output, platform discovery, workflow tutorials | Produce or export a usable artifact | Repeat projects and export completion |
| Internal/enterprise | Internal champions, training and role-based rollout | Target team completes the new workflow | Adoption, task success and support burden |
| Games/communities | Gameplay or interaction proof, community events, store discovery | Complete the first meaningful session | Return sessions and community health |

1. Map discovery, evaluation, activation, repeat use and relevant upgrades or adjacent products. Use exact prerequisites and plan entitlements. Offer upgrades where the product already delivers value and where the next benefit is concrete.
2. Demonstrate one real task. Use a controlled demo with authorized materials and identify any staged data. Build task-based help for onboarding, repeating the core task, recovery, and advanced use.
3. Define the event, cohort, denominator, time window and source. Track downstream outcomes alongside clicks and guardrails such as opt-outs, refunds, support tickets or abandoned tasks. Choose a tracking method appropriate to your consent and analytics setup.
4. Collect evidence in a small, time-bounded experiment. Record the condition to stop, revise, or expand. Review results each week and keep claims current.
