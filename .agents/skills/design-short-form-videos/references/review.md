# Review

Start with the most consequential defect in the requested deliverable. Give the exact beat, the observed problem, and the smallest useful edit.

## Scope the review to the current artifact

| Stage | What evidence can support |
| --- | --- |
| Script | Opening in isolation, end-to-start wording, the written payoff, factual scope and source fidelity. |
| Storyboard | Planned composition, cue visibility in available stills, timing intent and transition feasibility. |
| Rough cut | Observed picture, sound and captions in the rough cut that was actually inspected. |
| Export | Picture, sound and captions in the exact encoded delivery file. Use a versioned path or hash and identify any extracts as derived from that file. Recheck affected findings after changes. |
| Published | Playback on the actual destination where observed and audience or performance outcomes from the available data. |

Log the stage, artifact/version and evidence for each material finding. For an inspected boundary, include the time or frame range, track and inspection method. Use **pass**, **fail**, **inconclusive**, **not checked**, or **not applicable** with a reason grounded in the current artifact. A failed essential check requires revision. An unresolved essential check leaves that part of the review pending. A silent export can have audio checks marked inapplicable once the stream layout confirms there is no audio. A missing export leaves encoded picture and sound unassessed.

Written plans and transcripts support wording and intent. Ordered decoded frames support frame-to-frame observations, and metadata supports timing. A claim about audible continuity needs listening. A claim about smooth motion in playback needs an observed temporal sequence. Agent critique can report what was observed or suggest a likely clarity problem. Viewer comprehension, preference, learning and voluntary replay require actual viewer evidence.

## Review the applicable layers

| Check | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| First frame and first beat | Start at frame zero, including once with the sound off. Confirm the subject is legible and the first cue directs attention to something visible. |
| Payoff and claims | Trace the opening promise to a completed answer. Check source timestamps, screen labels, speaker intent, and qualifications. Confirm the most important claim matches the evidence. |
| Caption and framing | Play at phone size. Check timing, key-shot visibility, text wrapping, caption contrast and interface clearance for the intended placement. |
| Replay join | Preview two copies back-to-back with and without sound where applicable. Check motion phase or intentional hold, scene and caption state, clipped speech or effect tails, and whether the first play answers the question. For a viewer test, ask what was noticed on the second play. |
| Export | When a rendered file exists, check dimensions, duration, audio, playback, and a few key frames using available local tools. Distinguish a storyboard review from rendered playback verification. |

For an optical puzzle, hand the clip to first-time viewers and ask what they saw before and after the reveal. For a spoken clip, ask what claim the speaker made. Use the replies to find confusion, then inspect the edit at that point.

For a private test of first impressions, show each viewer one opening before asking what they expect the video to explain. For a loop test, compare a clean ending and a replay treatment that share the opening and payoff. Ask what answer the viewer got on the first play, what they noticed on the next play, and whether they wanted another look. Record the asset versions and observations. Use [replay evidence guidance](hook-loop-evidence.md#interpret-replay-evidence) when evaluating published performance.

A finding can read `Export v3 · final 4 frames and first 4 frames · decoded frames · fail · a duplicate frame interrupts rotating motion · trim the duplicate endpoint and recheck the encoded join.`
