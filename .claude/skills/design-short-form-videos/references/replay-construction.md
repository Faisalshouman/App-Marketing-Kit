# Construct a replay join

Start with a complete first-play payoff, then choose what the viewer can gain on replay. A callback, intentional cut, seamless restart and clean ending are all available. Choose the simplest picture, speech and sound treatments that serve that outcome. A single cut can combine a reveal, an action match and an ambience loop. Use only the layers that matter for this cut.

| Source | Start with | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrated software, tech or AI demo | Record a sentence bridge when the opening still works alone. Return to the same verified screen state or use relevant animation over the cut. | Land on the demonstrated result and finish cleanly if a bridge would repeat setup or confuse the UI state. |
| Science or education | Use a prediction or explanation followed by a return to a clue the audience can now interpret. Mark reconstructions and preserve qualifiers. | Hold the resolved comparison, diagram or result. Keep pauses and meaningful transitions that help learners follow the steps. |
| Podcast or interview | Use a callback to a point the guest actually made. Keep the setup, speaker and qualifications in context. | Keep the original ending or use clearly attributed narration. |
| Gameplay, stream or comedy | Match a meaningful action, return to the early clue, or time an intentional impact reset. Preserve the real cause, sequence and reaction. | Let the outcome land and end cleanly when the real motion or chronology cannot support the join. |
| Designed motion or ambient footage | Try a natural cycle, periodic animation, or a loop of a stable local region. Choose ambience or a musical phrase that fits. | Use an intentional visible restart or pick another source interval if masks, reflections or rhythm make the repeat distracting. |

## Write and record the spoken boundary

Write `START`, `END` and the literal `END + START` in playback order. Read `START` alone as a cold open. Then read across the seam and check logic, pronoun references, speaker, tense, negation and qualifiers. For original narration, record the joined line as a single utterance and split at a natural word boundary, with room for alternate takes. A script can establish wording and grammar. Audition the actual recorded voice for cadence, clipped consonants, breaths, pitch and room tone. Compose in the delivery language and check the caption punctuation against what is heard.

| Illustrative structure | Ending into opening | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast | “We moved the model, but” → “the camera never moved.” | The demonstration proves the main camera was fixed. Label a separate behind-the-scenes camera clearly. |
| Cause | “The check fails because” → “the input is empty.” | Reproduce the failure and verify the claimed cause. The opening needs a visible or otherwise identifiable input. |
| Standalone prompt after a complete lead-in | “That brings us back to one question.” → “Why does the animation jump?” | The question makes sense cold. If making it an embedded question, use statement order, as in “why the animation jumps.” |

If the face, pose or lip sync gives away an unintended restart, use a relevant cutaway and carry the natural dialogue or room tone through it. A J-cut brings next-shot audio in before its picture. An L-cut carries previous-shot audio into the next picture. At the file boundary, build that overlap into the delivered cycle and check where the actual audio starts. [Cambridge on reason clauses](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/because-because-of) · [Cambridge on reported questions](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/reported-speech-indirect-speech) · [Carlson et al. on prosody](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19744940/)

## Build the picture

- **Natural cycle or periodic animation.** Match motion phase, direction and speed as well as position. Compare a few adjacent frames on each side. Include secondary elements such as shadows, camera motion, game HUD, captions and procedural effects. For uniform rotation over (N) frames, sample frames 0 through (N - 1) at (360n/N) degrees. A 120-frame rotation advances 3° per frame and ends at 357°. The subsequent 360° state belongs to the restart. An intentional static hold can use repeated frames. [Video Textures](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/schoedl-2000-vt.pdf) · [Blender Cycles documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/editors/graph_editor/fcurves/modifiers.html)
- **Action match or hidden cut.** Use enough source handles to select matching movement. Maintain camera direction, velocity, lighting and scene state. For a wipe or whip pan, align the foreground coverage or blur direction and timing. A graphic match or deliberate hard reset can be visible and still work for the story. Check the intended visual effect at normal speed. [TikTok transition examples](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/behind-the-camera-shooting-tips-and-transitions-to-make-your-tiktoks)
- **Offset and repair.** Split a continuous source into A then B and reorder as B then A. The outer wrap becomes the original A-to-B split. Repair the moved discontinuity inside the cut with a compatible blend or mask. Check both joins and account for any lost duration. If faces, text or objects ghost, use a different interval or technique. For a local loop, inspect mask edges and elements crossing frozen regions. Use morphs, reversals or generated video textures only when the treatment fits and cannot be mistaken for additional observed action or evidence. [Video Textures](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/schoedl-2000-vt.pdf) · [Local loop research](https://hhoppe.com/videoloops.pdf)

## Build the sound

- **Speech and ambience.** Audition speech, room tone, music and effects separately when stems exist, then listen to the final mix. A zero crossing is a candidate edit point. Check the surrounding envelope and stereo image. Match both channels together. Choose a fade by ear for the material. Equal-power can suit uncorrelated sources, and equal-gain can avoid a level bump for closely matched in-phase signals. Keep consonants and transients intact and listen for double attacks, coincident words or phase cancellation. [Audacity loop editing](https://support.audacityteam.org/music/working-with-audio-loops/making-audio-loops) · [Audacity fade curves](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/fade_and_crossfade.html)
- **Music and effects.** Line up the phrase and its harmony, including any pickup, before matching the beat. At constant tempo, duration in seconds is 60 × beats ÷ BPM. Sixteen quarter-note beats at 120 BPM take 8 seconds, or 240 frames at 30 fps and 384,000 samples at 48 kHz. That is a timing example. If beat and frame grids disagree, audition the intended edit and check the encoded timing. Let action sounds follow the actual motion. For reverb or delay, render repeated cycles and select a stable interior cycle or wrap the outgoing tail into the beginning at the original duration. Check dynamics such as ducking and compressor recovery. [Ableton on Render as Loop](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/managing-files-and-sets/)

## Check the encoded wrap

Check the **outgoing audio tail** separately from the restart. In the decoded export, locate the end of the final spoken word, including its last consonant. Inspect and audition the interval from there to the file boundary, then play the same ending into the next opening. Use a waveform or short-window levels to find brief bursts anywhere in the tail. A silent final sample or a small jump at the boundary can coexist with a loud burst earlier in the tail.

If a burst appears, isolate dialogue, source ambience, music and effects. Check the assembled dialogue stem too, since it may already contain a source pickup. Trim unwanted pickups before normalizing or mixing. Set intentional room tone relative to nearby dialogue and avoid loudness-normalizing a short ambience fragment on its own. If the ending should be silent, verify silence from the planned cutoff through the decoded file end. Recheck that the final consonant is intact and record whether the exact export was heard or only measured.

**Example.** A stray source pickup in the assembled dialogue stem was audible even though the final samples were silent. Trimming the stem at the intended word boundary and fading the music independently fixed the tail. Check the full outgoing interval as well as the restart.

Use FFprobe for stream timing and FFmpeg for decoded frames, signal flags and short review extracts. Frame differences, silence, frozen-frame and level signals locate spots to inspect. The same held image can decode to unequal pixel values after lossy compression. Compare movement and adjacent frames around the join, then check playback. When using FFmpeg `xfade`, its inputs require matching constant frame rates, time bases, dimensions and pixel formats. Inspect any conversion for duplicated or dropped frames. AAC priming and padding can affect repeat behavior depending on encoded timing and the player.

Inspect two consecutive plays of the exact export. If the cut appears clean in an editor but there is a restart pause in a player, join two or three decoded copies in a scratch timeline and check their internal boundaries separately from the player's single-file repeat. Use an already authorized player or placement view when available and record what was tested. Follow [stage-specific review](review.md) for evidence and unresolved checks.
